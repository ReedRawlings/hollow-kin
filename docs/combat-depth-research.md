# Making Turn-Based Combat Feel Non-Generic — Mechanic Survey

**Framing.** "Generic" turn-based combat has one failure signature: **every turn's best action is the same action**, and the player is executing an already-solved loop. Every system below is a device for making the *best action change* — by turn, by enemy, by party state, or by run. They fall into six families:

1. **Turn economy** — actions themselves become the resource you win/lose (Press Turn, Boost, Brave/Default)
2. **State machines on enemies** — enemies have exploitable phases you create (Break, Locks, Armour)
3. **Legible enemy intent** — the turn becomes a puzzle with a known answer (StS, Into the Breach)
4. **Turn-order as a manipulable object** — the queue is a battlefield (Grandia IP, Trails AT, Child of Light)
5. **Attrition across encounters** — the fight is not the unit of decision (Darkest Dungeon stress, Battle Brothers fatigue, your MP)
6. **Execution layer** — inputs, not just choices (timed hits, Judgement Ring)

Your existing constraints already pre-select which of these are cheap and which are expensive. Called out per entry. Your **MP-does-not-regen** rule is your single strongest non-generic asset and is under-exploited — it's family 5, already built, currently only expressing itself as "cast less."

## 1. Turn Economy — actions as the currency

### 1.1 Press Turn (SMT III: Nocturne → SMT V)

**Mechanic.** Each side gets one Press Turn icon per living combatant. Every action spends one. Hitting a weakness, landing a crit, or *passing* converts a full icon into a **half icon** (act again); missing, or hitting an immunity/null/repel/drain, spends **extra** icons — repel/drain can end your whole turn. Half turns cannot generate further half turns, hard-capping a side at 2× its starting actions. If an action would both add and remove, **only the penalty applies**. ([megatenwiki.com/wiki/Press_Turn_System](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Press_Turn_System), [megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Press_Turn](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Press_Turn), [giantbomb.com/press-turn-system](https://www.giantbomb.com/press-turn-system/3015-2350/))

**Why it defeats generic.** Three effects, in order of importance:
- **Knowledge becomes economically valuable, not just numerically.** A weakness isn't "+50% damage," it's *a whole extra action*. That's a step-function reward for scouting, which makes your empty `resistances`/`weaknesses` and your Monsterpedia suddenly load-bearing instead of cosmetic.
- **It is symmetric.** Enemies gain turns off *your* weaknesses. This makes party composition a defensive liability, not just an offensive kit — and it makes a bad fight spiral visibly, which reads as tension rather than a slow HP subtraction.
- **Passing is a real move.** "Pass to convert a full turn to a half turn and let a better attacker act twice" is a genuinely interesting non-obvious action, and it costs nothing to implement.

**Failure modes.** (a) **Snowball asymmetry** — a boss with 4 icons that hits your weakness can chain 8 actions and delete a party before you act; SMT is famous for exactly this, and it reads as unfair when the player had no information. Nocturne's answer was cheap resistance-granting buffs and a fully-visible icon row. (b) **Solved fights** — once weaknesses are known, optimal play is mechanical; SMT patches this with enemies that change affinity mid-fight and with `Null` traps that punish auto-attacking a known weakness. (c) It **destroys any RNG-heavy design** — variance on top of turn-doubling produces unrecoverable swings.

**Fit with your game: strong, but modify.**
- Your **player-only crits** rule breaks symmetry perfectly here — crits generating half turns becomes a player-only privilege, which is a deliberate, readable asymmetry rather than an oversight.
- Your **3-creature locked party** means 3 base icons vs. up to 5 enemies. Straight Press Turn would have you outnumbered in actions 5-to-3 in a full room. Either decouple enemy icons from enemy count (fixed 3 for the enemy side regardless of bodies, scaling on floor depth) or the math is hostile. This is the main porting decision.
- **MP interaction is the best part.** Press Turn converts extra turns into extra *casts*, which drains the non-regenerating MP faster. That's a genuine push-your-luck knob: exploiting weaknesses wins the fight quicker but spends more MP per fight, so a strong Press Turn engine costs descent depth. That's a real tradeoff, not a strict upgrade — exactly what an attrition roguelite wants.
- **Auto-battle:** Press Turn is *the* system where auto-battle is easily made to feel smart, because a tactic ladder can visibly chain: your knowledge-fog rule means AUTO can only chain weaknesses on species already fought, so the first fight with a species looks clumsy and the rematch looks brilliant. That contrast is free narrative for your fog rule.

### 1.2 One More + Baton Pass + Technical (Persona 5 / Royal)

**Mechanic.** Weakness/crit grants **One More** — a second action for that actor only, not the party pool. Instead of acting, that actor can **Baton Pass** the extra action to an ally, who receives escalating multiplicative buffs per pass (Royal: 1.3×/1.5×/1.8× damage plus recovered SP at rank 3, and passes can loop the party). **Technical** damage triggers when you hit an enemy suffering a status with a specific damage type — Burn hit by Nuclear/Wind, Freeze hit by Physical, Shock hit by Physical, Sleep/Confuse/Fear/Despair/Brainwash hit by anything — dealing bonus damage *and*, in Royal, granting One More on its own. ([megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Baton_Pass](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Baton_Pass), [megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Technical](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Technical), [megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/One_More](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/One_More))

**Why it defeats generic.** Two independent things:
- **Baton Pass turns a party into a chain, not three parallel actors.** The buff stacking means the *order* you route the extra action through is the decision, and the last link deserves the biggest ability. This is the single cheapest way to make a fixed party of 3 feel like a combo machine — it creates emergent "who finishes" identity without any new stats.
- **Technical is a second, parallel weakness axis that the player builds themselves.** It's the answer to "what if this enemy has no elemental weakness": you *apply* a status, then exploit it. Status effects stop being chip-damage filler and become setup. This is the highest-value idea in this entire report for your project.

**Failure modes.** (a) Royal's Baton Pass multipliers are widely considered **overtuned to trivialisation** — a rank-3 chain plus Technical makes most non-boss fights a single unbroken player turn, and the discussion boards are full of "Technical or Baton Pass?" being a non-question because you get both ([gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/260936-persona-5-royal/79264748](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/260936-persona-5-royal/79264748), [steamcommunity.com/app/1687950/discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1687950/discussions/0/5501743491358790250/)). (b) Technical's type→status table is **unlearnable without a UI hint**; P5R shows a "TECHNICAL" prompt on the ability list, and without that it's invisible depth. (c) Chains reward a *single* optimal party, which is a problem for a collector — P5 dodges it by letting you swap Personas mid-fight; you can't.

**Fit: very strong, and cheaper than Press Turn.**
- You already have **6 status effects** and **per-creature resistances**. Technical is a table lookup: `(status, damageType) → bonus`. It needs zero new state, and it immediately makes your six statuses non-redundant.
- It **solves your flat type chart problem from the other direction.** You currently have empty `resistances`/`weaknesses` on all 30 creatures — that's a content backlog. Technical is *authored once as a rule*, not 30 times as data, and it gives you exploitable weakness on day one even against a creature with no elemental weakness. Do Technical before you fill in the type chart.
- **Baton Pass fits a locked party of 3 better than it fits P5's rotating roster**, because the chain is always the same three creatures — so the player learns *their* chain, deeply, over a run. Combined with your run-locked party, "this trio's chain" becomes the run's identity.
- **MP attrition tension:** make Baton Pass restore a little MP to the passer (Royal rank 3 does exactly this with SP). Now chaining is the MP economy's release valve, and the skill ceiling of chaining directly buys descent depth. That's an excellent coupling: better play → deeper floor, expressed through the resource you already track.
- **Auto-battle:** Technical is easy to encode in a tactic ladder ("if any enemy has a status my kit can exploit, exploit it") and makes AUTO look intelligent. Baton Pass routing is harder — a greedy "pass to the highest-multiplier attacker with MP" heuristic is adequate and legible.

### 1.3 Boost + Break (Octopath Traveler 1/2/0)

**Mechanic.** Each unit gains 1 BP per turn (max 5 stored), spendable to either multiply a physical attack's hit count or amplify a spell. Each enemy displays a **shield count** and its weaknesses; hitting a weakness decrements the shield, and at zero the enemy is **Broken** — it loses its current *and* next turn and takes increased damage until it recovers. You cannot boost on the same turn you gain BP by defending. ([game8.co Octopath 2 Break & Boost](https://game8.co/games/Octopath-Traveler-2/archives/404228), [destructoid.com/break-and-boost-system-guide-octopath-traveler-2](https://www.destructoid.com/break-and-boost-system-guide-octopath-traveler-2/), [twinfinite.net Break & Boost](https://twinfinite.net/guides/break-and-boost-system-octopath-traveler-2-explained/))

**Why it defeats generic.** The **shield count separates "chip the shield" from "burst the window."** That creates a two-phase rhythm inside every fight, and the decision each turn is "am I spending or saving?" — BP is a *visible* savings account with a hard cap, so hoarding has an opportunity cost. Crucially, **Break steals enemy turns**, so shield-breaking is defensive and offensive simultaneously; the player is never choosing between survival and damage, which removes the boring turtle strategy.

**Failure modes.** (a) **The optimal line is often mechanical:** multi-hit weakness attack to shave shield, boost the nuke on the break turn, repeat. Octopath 2 patches this with enemies whose weaknesses change on phase transition and bosses that reshuffle shields. (b) It makes **multi-hit abilities strictly better than single-hit** unless carefully priced. (c) With 5 enemies each carrying a shield, break-focused play stalls out — Octopath keeps encounter sizes small.

**Fit: good, but it competes with Press Turn — pick one.**
- Shields are a per-enemy counter on your `CombatEngine`, cheap to add, and they give your **capture action** a natural hook: **capture chance/price improves massively on a Broken target.** That's the single best mechanical justification for capture existing in the turn economy rather than being a separate minigame, and your `Capture.ts` rite system is already the right shape to consume a "target was broken" flag.
- **1-5 enemies is a problem.** Five shields is five setup investments. Either only elites/bosses carry shields (recommended — it makes boss floors mechanically distinct, which your 5/10-floor boss cadence wants), or shields scale down hard for trash.
- **MP:** Boost is a *free* resource that lets you amplify without MP, which partly *undercuts* your MP scarcity. If you take Boost, make it amplify basic attacks only, so it's the low-MP alternative rather than an MP discount on spells.

### 1.4 Brave / Default (Bravely Default)

**Mechanic.** **Default** = defend and bank one BP. **Brave** = spend BP to take up to 4 actions this turn — and you may go into **negative BP (debt)**, taking the actions now and then standing helpless for that many turns to repay. ([gamespot.com/reviews/bravely-default-review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/bravely-default-review/1900-6415602/), [quartertothree.com Work smarter not harder with Bravely Default](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2014/04/14/work-smarter-harder-bravely-default/))

**Why it defeats generic.** It makes **defending an active, aggressive choice** — the classic dead button becomes the setup for a 4-action alpha strike. And the debt option is a genuine push-your-luck gamble at the turn scale: burst now and pray you killed it, or you eat free hits. It also creates a real fight-length decision: turtle for four turns then one-shot the room, versus grind it down.

**Failure modes.** Well-documented: **trash fights collapse into "Brave ×4 with your strongest AoE turn one, end of battle,"** which the game leans into with an explicit auto-repeat-last-turn button. Boss fights degenerate the other way into "Default four times, then burst" as a rote opening. The debt mechanic is also a **trap for new players** — going negative against a boss is usually fatal, so the interesting half of the system is the half you learn not to use.

**Fit: partial. Take the Default half, skip the debt.**
- You already have `defend`. Making Defend bank a resource is a ~20-line change that converts your least-used action into a real one. Given you have a **±3 buff stage system already**, the cheap version is "Defend grants a stage of something in addition to mitigating," which is less novel but has none of the burst-degeneration problem.
- Full Brave is dangerous with **MP that doesn't regen** — 4 actions in a turn means 4 casts, so bursting becomes a huge MP spike. That's arguably *good* (bursting costs depth) but it will make trash fights either free or ruinous with nothing between.

## 2. Enemy State Machines — creating the exploitable window

### 2.1 Weakness Break / Toughness (Honkai: Star Rail)

**Mechanic.** Enemies show a **Toughness bar** plus their weakness types; only damage of a weakness type depletes it (any type still does HP damage). Emptying it triggers **Weakness Break**: the enemy's action is delayed, and a type-specific Break DoT/effect fires scaled by the attacker's **Break Effect** stat. Toughness regenerates after the enemy acts again, so Break is a recurring window, not a one-time state. ([honkai-star-rail.fandom.com/wiki/Toughness](https://honkai-star-rail.fandom.com/wiki/Toughness), [gamerant.com/honkai-star-rail-weakness-break-toughness-elements-explained](https://gamerant.com/honkai-star-rail-weakness-break-toughness-elements-explained/), [iggm.com Break Effect explanation](https://www.iggm.com/news/honkai-star-rail-break-effect-toughness-break-explanation))

**Why it defeats generic.** Two ideas worth stealing independently of the rest:
- **Off-element damage still works, just doesn't break.** This is the crucial difference from Pokémon-style type charts: a wrong-element team is *slower*, not *stuck*. In a collector with a locked party and no mid-run swaps, that distinction is the difference between "suboptimal run" and "unwinnable run." Given you cannot swap creatures during a descent, **your type chart must be HSR-shaped, not SMT-shaped.**
- **Break is its own damage stat.** Break Effect creates a whole second build axis — a creature can be built to break rather than to nuke. For you, that's a way to make your nine roles differentiate without touching base stats (your CLAUDE.md flags that nine Mages are numerically identical; a Break-oriented stat is a role-differentiator that costs no new stat blocks).

**Failure modes.** (a) The team-building meta collapses to **"do I have the element this boss is weak to"** — HSR mitigates with elements on every character but it's a real complaint, and it's exactly the trap a locked party amplifies. (b) Toughness bars on trash mobs are noise; HSR effectively ignores them there. (c) It requires substantial UI real estate per enemy — bar, weakness icons, break state.

**Fit: strong. This is probably your safest type-chart architecture.** Per-creature weaknesses (which you already have as a design rule) + toughness = every fight has a "who can break this" question answered differently per encounter, without any fight becoming impossible for the wrong trio.

### 2.2 Locks (Sea of Stars)

**Mechanic.** When an enemy begins charging a big attack, it displays a set of **Locks** — icons showing specific damage types (sword, blunt, sun, moon…) and a turn countdown. Breaking all locks before the timer cancels the attack entirely; breaking some weakens it proportionally. Locks change per cast, so the required combination is re-solved every time. ([seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat), [gamedatacentral.com Sea of Stars combat explained](https://gamedatacentral.com/guides/sea-of-stars-combat-system-explained-timed-hits-and-locks), [steamcommunity.com/app/1244090 breaking locks discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1244090/discussions/0/3817418065222117562/?ctp=2))

**Why it defeats generic.** It is the **best-designed marriage of enemy intent and weakness exploitation in a modern JRPG.** The enemy tells you exactly what it will do *and* exactly what combination of your abilities defuses it, and the combination changes, so the answer is a fresh multi-turn puzzle every cast — and the party's whole action budget for 2-3 turns is the puzzle's currency. It also makes damage *types* matter without any resistance multipliers at all, which is the key insight: **you don't need a type chart to make types matter; you need types to be keys.**

**Failure modes.** (a) Sea of Stars' own combat is criticised as **too easy overall** — locks are the good idea, but with generous timers and few real losses, defusing is routine rather than tense. (b) Locks requiring a type nobody in the party has is a **dead puzzle** — feels arbitrary. (c) It only fires on charged attacks, so ordinary turns are still plain.

**Fit: excellent, and unusually cheap for you.**
- You need no toughness bars, no turn-economy rewrite. A boss/elite gets `chargingAttack: { locks: DamageType[], turnsRemaining }`, your combat log already knows damage types, and the reward is that **boss floors feel structurally different from trash floors** — which your 5/10-floor cadence promises and currently doesn't deliver.
- **MP interaction is ideal.** Defusing costs MP you cannot get back, so the choice is "spend three casts to cancel the attack, or eat it and save MP for the next four floors." That is a genuinely hard decision and it is *only* hard because MP doesn't regen. Locks may be the single mechanic that most directly monetises your existing attrition rule.
- **Locks fix your dead-type problem** on 30 creatures with empty resistance data: authoring lock combinations for ~6 bosses is trivial content compared to authoring a 30×N resistance matrix.
- **Locked party caveat:** guarantee any lock set is defusable by *some* subset of any legal trio, or partially-defusable at worst. Sea of Stars' partial-break scaling is the safety valve — copy it.

### 2.3 Physical/Magic Armour (Divinity: Original Sin 2)

**Mechanic.** Statuses cannot land at all while the corresponding armour type is above zero: physical CC (knockdown, bleed) is gated behind Physical Armour, magical CC (stun, freeze, charm) behind Magic Armour. Armour regenerates out of combat but not naturally within it. ([steamcommunity.com/app/435150 armour system thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/435150/discussions/0/1495615865214005199/), [rpgcodex.net Divinity armor system discussion](https://rpgcodex.net/forums/threads/divinity-original-sin-2-armor-system-discussion.135649/))

**Why it was designed.** It removes save-or-suck RNG: no coin-flip stuns. Control is *earned* deterministically by stripping a resource.

**Failure modes — the most instructive negative example in this report.** It is broadly considered a design mistake by its own community, for reasons that generalise:
- **It forces mono-damage parties.** Splitting between physical and magic means stripping two bars instead of one, so mixed teams are strictly worse. A system meant to add tactics *reduced* team diversity.
- **It makes alpha-strike CC-lock the only viable strategy** — strip armour turn one, chain-CC forever, or lose. First-turn initiative decides fights.
- **Support/control classes are worthless until the very moment they become dominant.** Non-linear payoff curves feel bad in both halves.

**Fit: a warning, not a pattern.** Concretely: **do not gate your 6 status effects behind a resource that a 3-creature locked party can only strip by specialising.** If you gate statuses at all, gate them behind a *single* shared resource (the toughness/lock model), not behind matched-pairs. Your locked party makes DOS2's flaw strictly worse than it was in DOS2, where you built the party freely.

## 3. Legible Enemy Intent

### 3.1 Intents (Slay the Spire)

**Mechanic.** Every enemy displays an icon and, for attacks, the exact number and count of incoming hits before you act. Intents are drawn from a small per-enemy scripted/weighted move pool, so intent is *known* but the *next* intent is only partly predictable. ([slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Intent](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Intent), [pcgamer.com/best-design-2019-slay-the-spire](https://www.pcgamer.com/best-design-2019-slay-the-spire/))

**Why it defeats generic.** Franczyk's framing is the sharpest available: perfect information means **when you lose you cannot blame the black box**, so every turn becomes a solvable mini-puzzle nested in a larger strategic puzzle, and the loss is attributable to your own execution ([jeremiahgames.com — Perfect Information: The Killer Feature of Slay the Spire and Into the Breach](https://jeremiahgames.com/2019/03/04/perfect-information-the-killer-feature-of-slay-the-spire-and-into-the-breach/)). Mechanically, intent is what makes **defensive and pre-emptive actions non-generic**: "defend" is boring when incoming damage is unknown and interesting when you can see 22 damage coming and have 9 block available. Note the same article's caveat — perfect information alone didn't make StS good; it works because the surrounding possibility space is deep.

**Failure modes.** (a) Intent display **only creates decisions if you have reactive tools** — mitigation, pre-emptive kills, turn-order manipulation. Showing intent in a game where you only pick attacks adds information with nothing to do about it. (b) It exposes AI simplicity: once players learn the move pool, encounters are fully solved, and StS compensates with volume of enemies and deck variance. (c) It makes **any hidden RNG feel like a betrayal** by contrast.

**Fit: high value, and it's a prerequisite for several other entries here.**
- Your **random enemy targeting** rule collides with intent: showing "will attack" but not *whom* keeps some tension, but showing the target and keeping it random is incoherent. Either commit the target when the intent is displayed (recommended — random *selection*, telegraphed *result*, so the randomness is upfront and the decision is informed), or show target-unknown intents and accept weaker decisions.
- Intent is what makes your **`defend`** and your **buff/debuff stages** worth using. Right now a −ATK debuff is an abstract good; with intent it becomes "that 40-damage hit becomes 28, which my Tank survives." Same code, much better decision.
- Intent + **capture** is a great pair: seeing an enemy is about to flee/enrage/heal makes the capture-versus-kill decision timed.
- **Auto-battle:** intent is the raw material for tactic ladders that look smart. Your `TacticsAI` currently reasons from party state; giving it enemy intent lets "Fight Wisely" pre-emptively heal *before* the hit instead of after, which is the single most visible upgrade in perceived AI intelligence.

### 3.2 Full determinism (Into the Breach) and calibrated determinism (Invisible Inc.)

**Mechanic.** Into the Breach shows every enemy's target tile and damage before your turn; there is no to-hit roll, and each turn is a closed puzzle where the goal is damage *prevention*, not enemy elimination. ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach), [gamedeveloper.com — Road to the IGF: Into the Breach](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-), [cliqist.com interview with Justin Ma](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/06/into-the-breach-building-a-better-mech-with-subset-games-justin-ma/)) Invisible Inc. is deterministic in the *combat/stealth* layer — guard vision cones and patrol paths are fully visible and outcomes are certain — and pushes all randomness up to the *procedural generation and mission draw* layer. ([shacknews.com Invisible Inc programmer interview](https://www.shacknews.com/article/89531/invisible-inc-programmer-discusses-design-stealth-and-procedurally-generated-stages), [pentadact.com — What Works And Why: Invisible Inc](https://www.pentadact.com/2014-12-29-what-works-and-why-invisible-inc/))

**The transferable principle.** Both games **put the randomness in the setup and the determinism in the resolution.** Randomised layout/loadout/enemy composition creates novelty; deterministic resolution creates mastery. The generic-feeling JRPG does the opposite — same encounter every time, but resolved with hit rolls and damage variance, so the fight is both repetitive *and* unaccountable.

**Failure modes.** (a) Full determinism makes each turn a **solvable optimisation**, which becomes analysis paralysis and, at high difficulty, "there is exactly one correct line" — Into the Breach is regularly described as a puzzle game rather than a tactics game, and some players find that sterile. (b) Determinism raises the design cost of every unit enormously, because nothing is smoothed by variance. (c) There is genuine hidden RNG in ITB (spawn selection), and players notice ([youtube.com — Does Into The Breach Really Have Perfect Information?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNFURLFsd2E)).

**Fit: this is an architectural recommendation, not a mechanic.** You are a roguelite: your randomness budget is *already* being spent well (floor generation, encounter pools, band composition, item shops, wipe-loss). So **spend less of it inside the fight.** Concretely: your **player-only crits** are currently an in-fight variance source with no decision attached — consider making crits *conditional and deterministic* (crit vs. a Broken enemy, crit at the end of a Baton chain, crit when the target has a specific status) rather than a probability. Same feel, full accountability, and it converts a random number into a thing the player *does* — which is the entire trick.

## 4. Turn Order as a Manipulable Object

### 4.1 IP bar with cancel and counter (Grandia / Grandia II)

**Mechanic.** A visible bar carries all combatants; icons advance at speed determined by IP. At the **ACT/Action point** you commit an action, then continue advancing to the **execution point** — and during that window you are **vulnerable to having the action cancelled** by being hit, losing both the action and bar position. Fast weak attacks (Grandia's "Combo") advance you quickly; strong attacks ("Critical") are slower but *cancel* the target. Items and spells shift IP directly. ([grandia.fandom.com/wiki/IP_bar](https://grandia.fandom.com/wiki/IP_bar), [codeproject.com — Grandia's initiative bar](https://www.codeproject.com/Articles/612003/Grandias-initiative-bar), [lparchive.org/Grandia — Mechanics Sperging 2](https://lparchive.org/Grandia/Update%2007/), [steamcommunity.com/app/330390 what makes the combat](https://steamcommunity.com/app/330390/discussions/0/485623406957183181/))

**Why it defeats generic.** Grandia's system is widely held to be the best turn-based combat ever shipped, and the reason is **the cancel window**. It converts turn order from a display into a *target*. Every turn asks: is there a bigger threat mid-cast that I can interrupt, and is interrupting it worth more than damage? That's a second decision axis layered on the same action set, and it makes SPD a strategic stat rather than a stat that makes you go first. It also makes big enemy attacks **preventable by skill**, which is the difference between a boss that is a damage race and a boss that is a fight.

**Failure modes.** (a) **Presentation cost is high** — real-time bar, cast windows, animations that must be interruptible; this is not a menu system. (b) It leans toward one dominant strategy: keep everything permanently cancelled, which trivialises enemies with long casts. (c) Cancel-locking is genuinely unfun to receive; enemies that cancel *you* are the most hated part.

**Fit: high value, moderate cost, and it needs your SPD ordering to become continuous.** You have SPD-ordered turns, which is a sorted list, not a bar. The cheap version: keep discrete turns, but let abilities have a **1-turn cast delay** shown in the turn queue, and let specific abilities/statuses **push a queued actor back or cancel a queued cast**. You get 80% of the tension (interrupt the big one) for a fraction of the work, and it makes your ±3 SPD stages meaningful — an SPD debuff isn't "slightly worse," it's "you no longer act before the boss's charge resolves."

### 4.2 AT bar with battle order bonuses (Trails / Kiseki)

**Mechanic.** The AT bar shows the upcoming turn order, and **individual future slots carry randomly-assigned bonuses** — HP/EP/CP recovery, damage boosts, guaranteed critical, guaranteed status, even an extra turn. Players manipulate the queue to land their own units on good slots and deny enemies bad ones: arts take an extra turn to cast (so casting shifts your position), S-Breaks interrupt the queue outright, and various crafts delay or advance units. ([kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_order_bonus](https://kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_order_bonus), [kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Action_Time_Battle](https://kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Action_Time_Battle), [kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_mechanics/Sky](https://kiseki.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_mechanics/Sky))

**Why it defeats generic.** This is the **cheapest high-impact idea in this report for your codebase.** It creates a fresh tactical objective every single fight without touching abilities, stats, or enemies: the queue itself becomes contested terrain. And it directly attacks your worst problem — **a bonus slot that reads "restore 30 MP" is enormous in a game where MP does not regen.** Landing your Mage on that slot is worth reorganising the whole turn around, and denying the enemy healer the same slot is worth an attack that does no damage.

**Failure modes.** (a) It is **RNG-assigned**, so a run of bad bonus placement can decide a fight — Trails' answer is that bonuses are visible several turns ahead, so the randomness is information you plan around rather than a surprise. (b) It's a **numbers game the player can ignore** entirely and still win normal fights; it only becomes essential at high difficulty, so many players never engage. (c) Too-strong slots (guaranteed crit, extra turn) make fights lurch.

**Fit: outstanding. Recommend prioritising.**
- Implementation is a decoration on your existing sorted turn queue plus a few effects that swap/delay positions. No new combat state machine.
- **MP-restore bonus slots are the pressure-release valve your attrition economy needs.** Right now MP scarcity has one strategy (don't cast). Bonus slots give it a *second* strategy (fight for the refill), which converts a restriction into a puzzle. This is the mechanic most tightly matched to your specific ruleset.
- **Capture synergy:** a "guaranteed status" or "reduced capture cost" slot makes the capture attempt a timed opportunity rather than a spam.
- **Auto-battle:** slot bonuses are trivially legible to a tactic ladder (`if next slot has MP bonus and I'm the lowest-MP unit, act now`) and, importantly, are visible to the *player* watching AUTO — so the player can see the AI making a good decision, which is what "auto-battle that still feels like a decision" actually requires.

### 4.3 Timeline (Child of Light) and proximity (Like a Dragon: Infinite Wealth)

**Child of Light** puts a visible timeline with a **Cast** segment at the end; being struck during Cast delays or interrupts, and light-based spells (Slow/Haste, party-wide) exist mainly to shove units along it. Reviews single the timeline out as the standout system in an otherwise slight game ([rpgsite.net Child of Light review](https://www.rpgsite.net/review/3512-child-of-light-review), [thesixthaxis.com Child of Light review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2014/04/28/child-of-light-review/), [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_of_Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_of_Light)). It's the accessible, menu-friendly version of Grandia — closest to what you could ship.

**Infinite Wealth** added free repositioning within a radius, proximity bonuses, knockback into other enemies and environmental objects, and back-attack bonuses, specifically to answer the complaint that *Yakuza: Like a Dragon*'s turn-based combat was positionally inert ([gamerant.com — 6 things Infinite Wealth does better](https://gamerant.com/infinite-wealth-things-the-sequel-does-better/), [gamesurve.com — How to position in combat in Infinite Wealth](https://www.gamesurve.com/post/how-to-position-in-combat-in-like-a-dragon-infinite-wealth), [thegamer.com combat tips](https://www.thegamer.com/like-a-dragon-infinite-wealth-combat-tips-tricks/)). **Fit: poor** — this needs a spatial battlefield you don't have.

### 4.4 Ranks (Darkest Dungeon)

**Mechanic.** Four fixed party ranks front-to-back; every skill declares which ranks it can be used *from* and which it can *target*. Shuffle effects, corpses blocking advancement, and pull/push moves make position a contested state. ([steamcommunity.com/app/262060 how do positions work](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/1480982338952613328/), [dissectinggamedesign.substack.com — Darkest Dungeon and the Corpses Problem](https://dissectinggamedesign.substack.com/p/darkest-dungeon-and-the-corpses-problem))

**Why it defeats generic.** Rank is **positioning without a grid** — a single integer per unit that makes team composition a spatial puzzle and makes disruption (shuffle, stun, corpse) genuinely threatening rather than a minor tempo loss. The corpses mechanic is the instructive detail: it was added *specifically* because killing the front enemy previously let your back-rank targets slide forward for free, which trivialised rank-based defence — a good example of a positioning system needing an anti-collapse rule.

**Failure modes.** (a) Shuffle effects are the most complained-about mechanic in the game, because losing position is a **compounding, un-fixable loss** — several of your party's skills become unusable at once. (b) Rank restrictions **narrow viable party compositions hard**, which is bad for a collector.

**Fit: cheap (3 ranks = one int per creature) but risky with a locked party.** With 3 creatures locked for a whole descent, a rank-restricted kit means one bad shuffle degrades the run for multiple floors. If you take ranks, make repositioning cheap (a free action) so position is a tempo cost, not a lockout.

## 5. Attrition Economies — the fight is not the unit of decision

| Game | Mechanic | Design reason | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Darkest Dungeon** — Stress | A second HP bar that only goes up in dungeons, fills from crits/darkness/events, and at 100 rolls Affliction (Paranoid/Selfish/Hopeless — the hero acts against your orders) or, rarely, Virtue. Reduced only by town facilities with limited slots. Devs' stated goal: model *whether* a hero wants to fight, not just whether they can; they deliberately switched from hidden to fully-visible meters for feedback clarity. ([gamedeveloper.com — Deep Dive: Darkest Dungeon's Affliction System](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system), [darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Stress](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Stress), [nicolaluigidau.wordpress.com — Dynamics of Stress in Darkest Dungeon](https://nicolaluigidau.wordpress.com/2024/02/06/the-dynamics-of-stress-in-darkest-dungeon/)) | Creates a **second failure axis** so "winning every fight" isn't the same as "the run is fine," and drives the roster-rotation loop rather than a single favourite party | Widely called too punishing; loss of unit control is the most contentious part ([steamcommunity.com/app/262060 stress too deadly](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/458604254456092276/)). Stress also punishes *good* play (a hero crit still stresses you), which reads as arbitrary |
| **Battle Brothers** — Fatigue | Every action costs Fatigue; it recovers a small fixed amount per turn; at max the unit can only do nearly nothing. Armour weight raises the floor. Devs' own forums debate the "fatigue tax" repeatedly. ([battlebrothers.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_Mechanics](https://battlebrothers.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_Mechanics), [battlebrothersgame.com — Fatigue tax](https://battlebrothersgame.com/forums/topic/fatigue-tax/), [battlebrothersgame.com — Fatigue mechanics: bug or feature](https://battlebrothersgame.com/forums/topic/fatigue-mechanics-bug-or-feature/)) | **Intra-fight** attrition: the opening turns and the closing turns of a fight play differently, so a long fight has an arc rather than a repeated pattern | Fatigue becomes the single dominant stat, flattening build diversity ([steamcommunity.com/app/365360 is fatigue too important](https://steamcommunity.com/app/365360/discussions/0/1777136225020069325/)); it also makes heavy armour a trap for new players |
| **Slay the Spire** — Energy | 3 energy per turn, no carryover, cards cost 0-3. Refunds unspent nothing. | Forces a **per-turn packing puzzle**; the 0-cost card is a design lever, and "energy relics" are the highest-value reward tier because they change the puzzle's shape | Energy is not attrition at all — it resets. Compare to your MP: StS's tension is *within* a turn; yours is *across* floors. Different problem, don't copy the solution |
| **Hades** — Boons | Per-run god boons stack multiplicatively; Chaos boons impose a temporary curse (e.g. reduced damage for N encounters) in exchange for a permanent-for-the-run blessing; **Pact of Punishment** lets the player stack difficulty modifiers (Extreme Measures, Jury Summons adding an extra enemy, Middle Management adding a second miniboss, Tight Deadline) for reward multipliers ([hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment), [rpgsite.net — Pact of Punishment, Heat and modifiers](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/10287-hades-pact-of-punishment-heat-modifiers-and-how-to-maximize-your-rewards), [gameskinny.com Pact of Punishment guide](https://www.gameskinny.com/tips/hades-pact-of-punishment-conditions-bounties-heat-explained/)) | Chaos boons are the **debt pattern**: pay now in weakness, collect later — which is precisely a roguelite-scale version of Bravely's debt without the turn-scale degeneration. Pact is **player-authored variance**, which sidesteps the "run modifiers feel imposed" complaint | Multiplicative stacking makes runs bimodal (unstoppable or dead by biome 2); Pact modifiers are opt-in so most players never see the variety |

**Fit — read this as your central opportunity.** You have an attrition resource (MP, no regen) with **only one interaction: spend it or don't.** Darkest Dungeon and Battle Brothers both show that an attrition resource only generates decisions when there are *multiple competing ways to convert it and to recover it*. Concretely, the highest-leverage additions to your game are the ones that give MP more verbs:
- **MP-restoring turn-queue bonus slots** (Trails) → MP becomes contested
- **Baton Pass returning MP** (P5R) → skill converts into MP
- **Locks costing MP to defuse** (Sea of Stars) → MP converts into survival, not just damage
- **A "burn HP to cast" option** → MP and HP become exchangeable, so a low-MP party is *changed* rather than *disarmed*, which matters enormously for a locked party that can't rotate out
- **Defend banks something** (Bravely) → a zero-MP action becomes a real play

Also note the DD lesson against your **wipe = lose one random item** rule: it's a good rule, but stress/affliction shows that the most memorable attrition is the kind that **changes how a unit behaves**, not the kind that subtracts from an inventory. A creature that returns from a wipe with a temporary condition would be more memorable than a lost consumable, and it would give your `tombstone`/retirement machinery more to say.

## 6. Execution Layer — inputs, not just choices

| Game | Mechanic | Design reason | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Super Mario RPG / Paper Mario** — Action Commands | Timed button press on attack and on **defence** (Guard reduces damage; TTYD's **Superguard** on a tighter window nullifies damage *and* counters, at the cost of losing badge-based defence bonuses) ([mariowiki.com/Action_Command](https://www.mariowiki.com/Action_Command), [papermario.fandom.com/wiki/Action_Commands](https://papermario.fandom.com/wiki/Action_Commands)) | Keeps attention on **the enemy's turn**, which is where generic combat loses the player entirely. | **Legend of Dragoon** — Additions | Multi-step timed input chains per attack; failing a step aborts the combo mid-way; chains level up with use, unlocking longer ones | Turns the basic attack into a skill expression with a progression curve — the *non-MP* action stays interesting, which matters hugely in an MP-scarce game | Punishing (whiff = lost turn), and the chains become muscle memory, i.e. mandatory but no longer a decision |
| **Shadow Hearts** — Judgement Ring | A rotating needle over segmented arcs; hit each segment for a successful action, hit the small red **strike area** for bonus damage; miss and the action fails. Rings differ by ability and can be modified by items/accessories (widen, shrink, hide, speed up) ([shadowhearts.fandom.com/wiki/Judgement_Ring](https://shadowhearts.fandom.com/wiki/Judgement_Ring), [dualshockers.com — Shadow Hearts' Judgement Ring Made JRPG Turns Less Boring](https://www.dualshockers.com/shadow-hearts-judgement-ring/)) | The best version of this family because the **ring itself is a modifiable stat** — items that change your ring are progression that alters *execution difficulty*, not numbers. That's a genuinely rare design idea | Failure feels like a controller problem, not a decision problem; later games added an auto-ring accessory, and a notable share of players just used it ([cheapassgamer.com — does anyone else just auto ring it](https://www.cheapassgamer.com/threads/shadow-hearts-does-anyone-else-just-auto-ring-it.184953/)) |

Adds engagement without adding a punishment or an accessibility wall | Because there's no downside, it becomes an ambient tax on attention rather than a decision |

**Fit: the whole family conflicts with your auto-battle pillar, and that conflict is fatal-ish.** If AUTO exists and plays optimally, timed inputs are either (a) mandatory, which makes AUTO strictly worse and thus a trap, or (b) optional, which makes them pointless. The Shadow Hearts auto-ring history is the empirical evidence.

If you want any of it, the only clean version for your architecture: **upside-only timing on the basic attack, and AUTO takes the average result.** That gives manual play a small edge (justifying the existence of manual play in an auto-battle game — a real problem you have) without making AUTO a trap, and it specifically buffs the **MP-free action**, which is exactly the action you want to be attractive in a no-regen economy. Skip defensive timing entirely; it can't coexist with AUTO.

## 7. Combo / Chain Systems

**Chained Echoes — Overdrive.** A shared bar with coloured zones. Landing in the green **Overdrive** zone gives the party bonus damage and reduced damage taken; overshooting into red gives increased damage taken and higher MP costs. The bar moves *up* when you attack and *down* when you use the ability class the game currently displays as favoured — and that favoured class **rotates during the fight**. Reviews describe it as replacing MP-attrition and item-grinding with an in-fight tempo puzzle ([gamerant.com — Chained Echoes: how to lower overdrive](https://gamerant.com/chained-echoes-lower-overdrive/), [sifter.com.au — an almost perfect modernisation of classic JRPG gameplay](https://www.sifter.com.au/post/chained-echoes-matthias-linda-jrpg-pc-switch-review), [superjumpmagazine.com Chained Echoes review](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/chained-echoes-a-review/)).

*Why it's the most interesting entry in this section:* it makes **variety mandatory**. You cannot spam the best ability, because spamming pushes you into red; you must rotate through your kit to chase the changing favoured class. That is a direct structural answer to "generic combat = same action every turn," and it needs no enemy design at all. Failure mode: it's opaque on first contact, and Steam threads show a real split on whether it's tactical or just an annoying meter to babysit ([steamcommunity.com/app/1229240 is the combat still dog](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1229240/discussions/0/591775366372140789/)).

**Fit: very strong and *directly relevant* to your specific weak spot.** Your CLAUDE.md notes nine Mages are numerically identical and that fights may feel samey. Overdrive makes *ability variety within a creature's kit* the thing the combat rewards, which extracts more distinctness from the abilities you already have (31 of them) without authoring new creatures. **Caution:** Chained Echoes explicitly uses Overdrive *instead of* MP attrition. Running Overdrive *and* non-regenerating MP means two overlapping "don't spam" systems — you'd likely need to soften one. The version I'd try: Overdrive's rotating favoured class, but the reward is **MP discount / MP refund** rather than damage, so the two systems compose instead of competing.

**Octopath Boost, Persona Baton Pass** — covered above (§1.2, §1.3).

**Monster Sanctuary** — the closest genre neighbour. 3v3, and attacks are internally multi-hit, building a **combo counter that multiplies subsequent damage in the same turn**, so ability *ordering* within a round is the core skill; buffs and heals also add stacks so defensive play still builds the combo. The reviewer's stated reason it doesn't go stale is that **weaknesses are deliberately non-intuitive** — fire monsters aren't reliably weak to water, and some creatures are weak to damage *they themselves deal* ([turnbasedlovers.com Monster Sanctuary review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/monster-sanctuary-2/), [rpgfan.com/review/monster-sanctuary](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/monster-sanctuary/), [metroidvaniareview.com/monster-sanctuary](https://metroidvaniareview.com/monster-sanctuary/)).

**Fit: highest of any game here — copy the structure.** Same party size, same collector framing, same non-archetype weaknesses (which is literally your "no archetype-level type chart" rule). Two direct takeaways: (1) your per-creature weakness rule is *validated* — and the reason it works is that it's non-inferable, so the Monsterpedia is the reward for engagement; (2) **an intra-round combo counter is the cheapest possible way to make ordering matter with a 3-creature party**, and it's compatible with everything else here.

**Trails S-Craft / orders** — see §4.2. The relevant extra: S-Breaks **interrupt the turn queue outright**, so a charged ultimate is a tempo tool, not just damage.

**Yakuza Heat** — a bar built by combat that unlocks contextual finishers; in *Like a Dragon* it became MP-style skill points plus proximity-triggered bonus attacks. Fit: weak; the interesting part is the contextual/environmental trigger, which needs the space you don't have.

## 8. Auto-Battle That Still Feels Like a Decision

This is the hardest problem in your design, because most of §1-§7 assumes a human deciding each turn. Prior art:

**Dragon Quest tactics.** Per-character standing orders (Fight Wisely / Show No Mercy / Focus on Healing / Don't Use MP / Follow Orders) set outside combat. The DQIII HD-2D discussion is instructive: "Fight Wisely" is genuinely good, and the community position is roughly *use tactics for trash, Follow Orders for bosses* ([wccftech — Don't sleep on the "Fight Wisely" auto-battle option](https://wccftech.com/how-to/dragon-quest-iii-hd-2d-remake-dont-sleep-on-the-fight-wisely-auto-battle-option/), [resetera — Fight Wisely or Follow Orders for normal battles](https://www.resetera.com/threads/fans-of-dragon-quest-and-other-similar-games-do-you-prefer-fight-wisely-or-follow-orders-for-normal-battles.1035222/), [gamerant — how to command your party in DQ3 remake](https://gamerant.com/dragon-quest-3-remake-how-control-command-all-party-members-tactics-switch/)).

**The load-bearing insight from that split: auto-battle is not a difficulty setting, it's a *pacing* setting.** It exists so trash floors don't cost attention. Which means: **the decision should not live in the turn, it should live in the tactic assignment and in the choice of when to override.** Your architecture already does this correctly (per-creature persisted tactics, plus `follow_orders` creatures still prompting manually while AUTO is on — that mixed mode is the best idea in your combat code and it's the exact right answer).

**FF12 gambits.** A programmable priority list per character (`Ally: HP < 70% → Cure`), evaluated top-down. Reviled by some for "the game plays itself," defended by others as the only party-AI system that ever actually solved party AI, since the player *authors* the behaviour ([resetera — why was the gambit system so reviled](https://www.resetera.com/threads/i-really-dont-get-why-the-gambit-system-of-final-fantasy-xii-was-so-reviled-considering-how-it-pretty-much-solved-the-drawbacks-of-partner-ais.152253/), [twinfinite — FFXII basically plays itself and it's amazing](https://twinfinite.net/ps4/final-fantasy-xii-is-basically-menu-ing-the-game-and-i-love-it/), [rpgsite.net FFXII review](https://www.rpgsite.net/review/2789-final-fantasy-xii-review)).

*Why it works when it works:* the decision moves **up a level** — from "what do I do this turn" to "what is my party's policy." Writing the policy is the game. That's the same move Invisible Inc. makes with randomness (push it to a different layer) and it's the correct move for you.

*Failure modes:* (a) the authoring UI is a programming task and most players won't do it; (b) once written, a good gambit set genuinely does play the game, which hollows out the combat you designed; (c) it exposes bad AI — anything the gambit language can't express, the player must micromanage, and that's where the frustration lives.

**Gacha auto.** Auto/repeat-battle is table stakes in the category ([ultimategacha — best gacha games with auto-battle](https://ultimategacha.com/best-gacha-games-with-auto-battle/), [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gacha_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gacha_game)). The relevant design fact: in those games **auto is deliberately weaker than manual**, and the *content* is gated so that you clear farmable stages on auto and bosses manually. The gameplay was moved out of the turn and into team construction. HSR does this most explicitly: auto-battle notoriously wastes ultimates and mistimes Breaks, so the Break system is precisely the thing auto cannot play well — that's how HSR keeps manual play meaningful while still shipping auto.

**Synthesis — the design rule you want.** *Auto-battle should handle the parts of combat that are about resource sufficiency; manual play should handle the parts that are about timing and windows.* Every mechanic in this report sorts cleanly under that rule:

| Mechanic | AUTO can play it? | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Weakness exploitation, Technical | Yes, well | Good for trash-floor pacing; your knowledge-fog rule makes AUTO visibly improve as the Monsterpedia fills — free progression feel |
| Turn-queue bonus slots | Yes, adequately (greedy) | Player *watches* AUTO grab an MP slot and reads it as intelligent |
| Break / Toughness | Poorly (needs burst timing) | Ideal boss mechanic — reason to switch off AUTO |
| Locks | Poorly (needs multi-turn planning) | Ideal boss mechanic |
| Baton Pass chains | Poorly (routing is combinatorial) | Manual skill expression |
| Timed hits | Not at all | Avoid, or upside-only with averaged AUTO result |
| Capture | Never — make it manual-only | Capture is the run's biggest decision; forcing manual for it is correct and free |

Concretely for you: **add a tactic that is about the attrition economy, not the fight.** "Fight Wisely" optimises the current battle; a tactic like *Conserve for the Descent* (never cast above X% of remaining MP, prefer basics, accept longer fights) makes the tactic ladder express the *roguelite* layer, which is a decision no existing DQ tactic makes. That's the differentiated version of your existing system, and it requires no combat changes at all.

## 9. Roguelite Encounter / Biome Variation Per Run

### 9.1 How the reference games do it

**Slay the Spire — acts as distinct rule-spaces.** Each act has its own enemy pool, elite pool, and boss set, plus escalating structure: Act 1 teaches, Act 2 introduces multi-enemy fights and status-inflicting enemies, Act 3 introduces very high HP and multi-phase threats, Act 4 is a single gated gauntlet. Card and relic reward *pools* also shift by act, and elites/bosses gate the highest-value rewards ([slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Acts](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Acts), [slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters), [slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Map_Locations](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Map_Locations)). StS2 adds **alternate acts** — the same depth slot can be one of several different acts with different pools and gimmicks, which is *composition* variance rather than *content* variance ([mobalytics.gg/slay-the-spire-2/guides/alternate-acts](https://mobalytics.gg/slay-the-spire-2/guides/alternate-acts), [sts2front.com/mechanics/alternate-acts](https://www.sts2front.com/mechanics/alternate-acts/)).

*The key point for you:* StS's acts differ by **the kind of problem posed**, not just enemy stat level. Your bands 1-2 currently share an identical pool and differ only in enemy *level* — your own docs flag this ("depth changes enemy level, not enemy variety"). Straight stat scaling is the definition of generic. The cheapest fix is not more creatures; it's **band-specific composition rules**: band 1 = 1-2 enemies, band 2 = 3-5 weaker enemies (which makes AoE and multi-target abilities suddenly relevant), band 3 = paired enemies with a support that must be killed first, etc. Composition is free content.

**Hades — biome modifiers + Pact.** Each biome has its own enemy set, hazard type (Tartarus none, Asphodel lava, Elysium none but elite duos, Styx poison + chamber-choice maze), and its own reward mix; Pact of Punishment then layers player-chosen modifiers on top (extra enemies, extra minibosses, timers, damage reduction) ([hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment), [primagames.com — Hades Pact of Punishment modifiers and heat levels](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-guide-pact-of-punishment-modifiers-heat), [hades.wiki.fextralife.com/New_Game_Plus](https://hades.wiki.fextralife.com/New_Game_Plus)).

*Key point:* Hades' biome identity is carried by an **environmental hazard**, not by enemies — the same skeleton is a different problem when the floor is lava. For a turn-based game with no space, the equivalent is a **floor-wide combat rule** (see 9.3).

**Risk of Rain 2 — Artifacts.** Opt-in global rule rewrites for the whole run: *Artifact of Command* (choose items instead of random), *Sacrifice* (no chests; enemies drop items), *Swarms* (double enemy count, half HP), *Glass* (500% damage, 10% HP), *Enigma* (only random equipment), *Kin* (each stage spawns only one monster family), *Honor* (only elite enemies spawn) ([riskofrain2.fandom.com/wiki/Artifacts](https://riskofrain2.fandom.com/wiki/Artifacts), [pockettactics.com/risk-of-rain-2/artifacts](https://www.pockettactics.com/risk-of-rain-2/artifacts), [primagames.com — Risk of Rain 2 artifacts guide](https://primagames.com/gaming/risk-of-rain-2-artifacts-guide-locations-codes-and-effects)).

*Two directly stealable ones:* **Kin** (a whole descent where every enemy is one archetype — instantly makes your 11 archetypes into 11 distinct run flavours, and makes party composition against a known archetype a real pre-run decision, which is exactly what your Party Select screen needs to be about) and **Swarms** (double enemies, half HP — changes the AoE/single-target calculus with zero new content).

**Monster Train — clan pairing + Covenant.** Run identity comes from choosing a primary and allied **clan**, and the combinatorics of the pairing (not from randomised enemies); **Covenant ranks** then stack escalating handicaps for higher difficulty ([monster-train.fandom.com/wiki/Covenant_Ranks](https://monster-train.fandom.com/wiki/Covenant_Ranks), [steamcommunity.com/app/1102190 best clan combo for Covenant 25](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1102190/discussions/0/2299597907732564805/), [frostilyte.ca — Monster Train 2 Covenant 10 guide](https://frostilyte.ca/2025/07/08/monster-train-2-gameplay-guide-for-winning-your-first-covenant-10-run/)).

*Key point:* Monster Train's variance is mostly **in the player's build space, not the enemy's.** Your locked 3-creature party is structurally identical to a clan pairing — so your run variance can and should come primarily from *which trio you take*, and that only works if trios interact combinatorially. Baton chains, combo counters, and archetype-themed floors all do that; independent stat blocks do not.

### 9.2 Games where an elemental/type theme rotates per run and buffs both sides

This is the specific thing you asked about, and it's rarer than the general biome-modifier pattern. The real prior art:

**Siralim Ultimate — Realm Properties (closest match, and it's a monster collector).** Enabling **Realm Instability** attaches N randomly-drawn properties to a realm. They are explicitly split into three buckets: properties that debuff *your* creatures (`Less Attack Damage: x%`, `Can't Dodge`, `Bad Luck` — random effects reroll and take the worse result), properties that buff *enemy* creatures (`Always Critical Damage`, `Extra [Stat]: x%`, `Good Luck` as the mirror of Bad Luck, `Reflect Damage Taken: x%`), and properties that reshape the realm itself (`Enemies Belong To Same Race` — i.e. Artifact-of-Kin, `Rapid Fatigue`). Realms additionally each have unique encounter tables, and Instability lets creatures spawn in realms they normally can't ([siralimultimate.wiki.gg/wiki/Realms](https://siralimultimate.wiki.gg/wiki/Realms), [siralimultimate.wiki.gg/wiki/Combat](https://siralimultimate.wiki.gg/wiki/Combat), [earlyguides.com/siralim-ultimate/combat](https://earlyguides.com/siralim-ultimate/combat)).

*This is the direct blueprint.* Note two design choices worth copying: properties are **drawn per realm, visible before entry** (so the player accepts them like a Pact), and the buckets are kept **separate rather than symmetric** — Siralim mostly does not use "both sides get the same buff," it uses "this realm is tilted, here's how." Symmetric buffs are the harder design (see below).

**Pokémon Mystery Dungeon — floor weather (the truest "buffs both sides" case).** Dungeon floors carry weather that applies to every Pokémon on the floor, ally and enemy alike: **Sunny** boosts Fire and weakens Water, **Rainy** boosts Water and weakens Fire, **Sandstorm** damages all non-Ground/Rock/Steel each turn, **Hail** damages all non-Ice, **Cloudy** reduces all non-Normal damage, **Fog** massively weakens Electric, **Snow** reduces visibility. Weather changes between floors and can be overridden by abilities and moves — so a creature with a weather-setting move or a weather-immune ability becomes situationally valuable ([bulbapedia — Weather (Mystery Dungeon)](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Weather_(Mystery_Dungeon)), [mysterydungeonwiki.com — Rescue Team: Weather](https://mysterydungeonwiki.com/wiki/Rescue_Team:Weather), [gamefaqs — PMD weather mechanics](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/929408-pokemon-mystery-dungeon-red-rescue-team/faqs/75111/weather-mechanics)).

*Why it's the best model for your tower:* it is **per-floor, symmetric, and type-keyed** — exactly the shape you described. And the design payoff is specific: a symmetric weather that boosts Fire doesn't just change your damage, it makes an enemy Fire-type *dangerous on that floor specifically*, so the same species is a different threat depending on where you meet it. **That is the cheapest possible source of encounter variety for a game with 30 creatures in 2 identical pools.** It also gives you a reason for weather-manipulating abilities to exist, which is a whole ability design space you currently don't have.

**Pokémon mainline — Terrain moves** are the same idea in miniature (Grassy/Misty/Electric/Psychic Terrain apply to all grounded combatants for 5 turns), and demonstrate the competitive-tested version: symmetric field effects are strategically rich because **whoever sets the field chooses when the mutual benefit favours them** ([bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Weather](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Weather)).

**Loop Hero** — the player *places* biome tiles that both spawn the enemies and grant the player stats, so buffing yourself and the opposition is literally the same action. Worth knowing as the purest expression of the pattern, though the mechanism (tile placement) doesn't port.

**Honkai: Star Rail — Simulated Universe** is the gacha-scale version: each run you pick a **Path** whose resonance/blessings reshape your kit, and world modifiers alter enemy composition and rules; the same combat system produces different-feeling runs entirely through per-run rule draws.

**Symmetric-buff failure mode to plan for.** A theme that buffs both sides is only interesting if the two sides can exploit it *unequally*. If your floor says "Fire damage +30% for everyone" and your trio has one Fire creature while the enemies have two, that's a straight difficulty spike with no decision. Mitigations, in order of value:
1. **Show the theme before the floor is entered**, so the room choice on your run map becomes the decision (this is where the theme creates gameplay — your `RunGenerator` already generates a descent the player picks through, so this is nearly free).
2. **Make exploitation asymmetric by design:** the theme buffs a *damage type*, and the player can choose *which of their abilities to use*, whereas enemy AI picks from a fixed kit. The player exploits a theme better than the AI does almost automatically — that's the built-in edge that makes symmetry fair.
3. **Pair every buff with a cost the player can dodge and the enemy can't** (Sandstorm-style chip damage on non-immune types → the player picks a trio that's immune, the enemies bleed).
4. **Never make a theme that can zero out a party's whole kit.** With a locked party and no mid-run swap, a "Fire nullified" floor against a Fire trio is a run-ender through no decision of theirs. Cap theme magnitude well below "unwinnable."

### 9.3 Concrete recommendation for your tower

Combining the above into the cheapest high-yield package for your codebase, since almost none of it requires new creatures:

- **Floor themes (PMD weather + Siralim realm properties).** Draw 0-1 themes per floor from a small pool, display on the run map before commitment. Symmetric type-boost themes, plus a few structural ones. Because your floor generation and map-preview already exist, this is data plus one modifier hook in `CombatEngine`.
- **Band-level composition rules (StS acts).** Stop scaling only enemy level. Band 1: 1-2 enemies. Band 2: 3-5 weak. Deeper: mixed with a support-priority target. This makes your existing 30 creatures produce genuinely different fights.
- **Descent modifiers (RoR2 Artifacts / Hades Pact).** A Gatekeeper-purchasable or boss-granted run modifier — `Kin` (single-archetype descent), `Swarms` (double enemies, half HP), `Honor` (elites only, better Obols). Player-chosen difficulty-for-reward is the pattern with the best track record and it slots directly into your existing Essence/Gatekeeper economy.
- **A single-archetype descent is your best variety-per-effort ratio.** 11 archetypes × existing content = 11 distinctly-flavoured runs, and it makes the Party Select screen a real decision for the first time.

## 10. Ranked Recommendations For *Your* Specific Design

Ordered by (impact on non-genericness) ÷ (implementation cost against your described architecture).

| # | Add | Why it's top of the list for you | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Technical damage table** (P5R) — `(status, damageType) → bonus + extra action` | Makes your 6 existing statuses non-redundant and gives you exploitable weaknesses *today*, without authoring a 30-creature resistance matrix. Authored once as a rule, not 30 times as data | Very low |
| 2 | **Turn-queue bonus slots, MP-flavoured** (Trails AT bonus) | The only mechanic here that turns your no-regen MP rule from a restriction into a puzzle. Decorates your existing sorted queue. Legible under AUTO | Low |
| 3 | **Enemy intent display** (StS) | Makes `defend`, your ±3 debuff stages, and pre-emptive play meaningful; upgrades `TacticsAI` visibly. Requires resolving the tension with random targeting (telegraph the randomly-chosen target) | Low-medium |
| 4 | **Locks on elites/bosses** (Sea of Stars) | Makes your 5/10-floor boss cadence structurally different rather than statistically different, and monetises MP scarcity as a genuine decision | Medium |
| 5 | **Floor themes, symmetric and type-keyed** (PMD weather / Siralim realm properties) | Highest variety-per-line-of-code available to you; makes the same 30 creatures different threats on different floors; the map-preview decision is already built | Low-medium |
| 6 | **Band composition rules** (StS acts) | Stops depth from being a pure stat multiplier. Pure data | Very low |
| 7 | **Intra-round combo counter** (Monster Sanctuary) | Makes *ordering* matter with only 3 creatures — the exact problem a locked trio has | Low |
| 8 | **Toughness/Break, or Boost — pick exactly one** (HSR / Octopath) | Second exploitable axis + the natural hook for making **capture** a timed window rather than a spam. Do not stack this with Locks and Technical all at once | Medium-high |
| 9 | **Baton Pass with MP refund** (P5R) | Turns the locked trio into a learnable chain; skill converts into descent depth | Medium |
| 10 | **`Conserve for the Descent` tactic** | Makes the auto-battle ladder express the roguelite layer, which no DQ tactic does. Differentiates your AUTO from prior art at almost no cost | Very low |

**Anti-recommendations.** (a) **Do not** gate statuses behind matched physical/magic resources (DOS2) — a locked trio makes its known flaw worse. (b) **Do not** add timed inputs on defence — irreconcilable with AUTO (Shadow Hearts auto-ring precedent). (c) **Do not** implement full Bravely debt — trash fights degenerate and the MP spike is savage in a no-regen economy. (d) **Do not** fill in the 30-creature resistance matrix as your next content task — a table-driven rule (Technical, Locks, or Toughness) delivers exploitable weakness sooner and doesn't need to be rebalanced 30 times. (e) **Do not** take more than two of §1's turn-economy systems; Press Turn, Boost, Brave and Baton Pass all solve the same problem and stacking them makes fights unreadable.
